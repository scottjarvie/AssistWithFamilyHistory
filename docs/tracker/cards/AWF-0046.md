## Why this exists

The Personal AI scenario this product is measured against is: build a
source-grounded ancestor branch, **resolve a conflict**, and create a family
story packet. Two of those three were real. The middle one was not.

Conflict is the ordinary condition of genealogy, not an edge case. A census
says 1847, a headstone says 1849, a parish register says 1848. The work *is*
deciding which record to believe and writing down why. A research tool that can
only say "these disagree" has handed the hardest part back to the person with no
place to record the answer.

## What is genuinely strong here

This was never a missing-foundation problem. The evidence model is the best-built
part of the vault and it is unusually rigorous for a product this young:

- `sources` (`convex/schema.ts:392`) carries `repository`, `url`, `author`,
  `publicationDate`, and a 14-value `type`.
- `citations` (`:455`) separates **evidence from conclusion** with `isEvidence`
  (`:460`), grades belief on a five-level `confidence` scale (`:465`), and keeps
  `extractedText` alongside `editedText` (`:474`) plus `accessDate` (`:479`).
- `citationLinks` (`:499`) is the fact→source pointer: a citation attaches to a
  person, relationship, event, or place, down to the specific `field` it
  supports (`:509`).
- `sourceFacts` (`:526`) are candidate facts born already attached to their
  citation, and they deliberately do not overwrite canonical person fields.

That is a real Source/Citation separation, which most hobby tools never build.
The gap was a missing *move*, not a missing *model*.

## Current truth

**Shipped 2026-08-17 on `claude/fh-conflict-visible`, verified `pnpm verify`
57/57.** The person half of the lifecycle is complete and the AI can now see the
thing it is being asked to help with.

- **The AI is told the truth about conflicts.** `unresolvedConflicts` in the
  context pack was assigned `storyClaimReadiness.unresolvedImportWarnings` — an
  array of warning strings — so an AI asked to help resolve a conflict received a
  list containing no conflicts. It now carries the `sourceFacts` rows the vault
  actually marked `status: "conflict"`, each with the source's reading, this
  vault's reading, the citation behind the disagreeing one with its confidence
  and cited text, and the reason it was flagged
  (`convex/contextPackBuilder.ts`). Import warnings keep their own honest name
  under `unresolvedImportWarnings`.
- **`citations.conflictsWith` is finally read.** Declared at
  `convex/schema.ts:483` and validated on write at `convex/citations.ts:81-89`
  since the model landed, it had no reader anywhere. The conflict export now
  resolves it, so the reading a researcher decided against travels beside the
  one they kept.
- **A conflict has a closing move.**
  `vaultMutations.resolveSourceFactConflict`, reached from the person workspace
  through `/api/operations/conflicts`, sets a conflicting fact to `accepted` or
  `rejected`, requires a reason of at least 20 characters, writes that reasoning
  to `researchLog` as durable research text, and closes the auto-opened
  `conflict_resolution` task once the last conflict on that person is settled.
  Nothing is deleted; the losing reading, its citation, and its source all stay.
  The person's canonical fact is **not** rewritten — believing a source reading
  is a statement about the evidence, and editing a recorded birth date remains a
  separate deliberate act.
- **An AI cannot settle one, and now genuinely cannot.** The boundary used to be
  accidental rather than enforced: `family_history_save_source_evidence` would
  have accepted a re-save of a conflicting fact with `status: "accepted"`
  (`convex/mcpFamilyHistory.ts`), which is resolution by another name.
  `mayAiSetSourceFactStatus` (`lib/vault/conflictResolution.ts`) now refuses any
  MCP write that takes a fact *out* of conflict. Flagging a conflict, and
  continuing to work on a conflicting fact, both still work. The refusal tells
  the AI what to do instead: propose on the `conflict_resolution` research task,
  with the evidence on both sides, and let the person confirm.

**Partial.** Proposal is possible but unstructured: an AI proposes by writing a
research finding or task note through `family_history_save_research_work`. There
is no dedicated proposal record, no side-by-side "your AI suggests X because Y —
accept or decline" surface in the person workspace, and no notification. That is
what AWF-WO-012 covers, and it is deliberately blocked on the decision below.

**Later.** General in-vault conflict detection between any two citations already
in the vault (nothing compares two citations today — detection runs only on the
FamilySearch capture-import path, `lib/familysearch/sourceFacts.ts:80-124`);
conflict types beyond name, birth, and death; and any FamilySearch-side conflict
resolution (`familySearchSync.syncStatus: "conflict"`, `convex/schema.ts:787`,
is declared with no detector behind it).

## Why Scott is needed

Everything above was safe to build because it either shows a person more of what
their own vault already knows, or *narrows* what an AI may do. The next step is
the first one that widens AI authority, and it changes a promise the product has
already made to people on the consent screen.

The `family_history:research:write` permission currently reads, in the person's
own consent language: **"Proposals only. It cannot accept a conclusion for
you."** Building an AI-side resolve tool would make that sentence false, and it
is not a sentence an implementation decision should quietly rewrite. Deciding
which of two records to believe is the researcher's judgment, and it is the part
of genealogy people most want to be theirs.

## Smallest decision or action

Answer one question: **may a chosen AI ever settle a conflicting source fact
itself, or only propose a resolution for you to confirm?**

## Recommendation

**Propose-and-confirm only.** Keep resolution a person's act permanently, and
spend AWF-WO-012 on making the proposal excellent — a structured proposal record
carrying the AI's reasoning and the evidence on each side, shown in the person
workspace as a one-click confirm or decline.

Two reasons. First, it keeps a promise already made rather than amending it.
Second, it is the higher-value product: the slow part of resolving a conflict is
not clicking a button, it is finding and weighing the records. An AI that does
that work and hands you a clear, sourced recommendation is doing the part people
cannot easily do themselves; an AI that silently picks a birth year is taking the
one part they wanted.

## Alternatives and trade-offs

1. **Propose-and-confirm only (recommended).** The AI reads conflicts, gathers
   evidence, and writes a proposal. You confirm. Cost: every conflict needs one
   human touch, so a vault with two hundred flagged facts needs two hundred
   confirmations. Mitigable with batch review, not with AI authority.
2. **AI may resolve, with an audit trail and undo.** The AI settles the conflict
   and the change is logged and reversible. Cost: the consent screen must be
   rewritten, and "reversible" is only true if people actually read the log —
   which, for a decision they never made, they will not. Genealogy's failure mode
   is confidently propagated wrong conclusions, and this adds a fast path to it.
3. **AI may resolve only within a narrow band** — e.g. only when its confidence
   is `very_high` and the losing record is `low` or `very_low`. Cost: the band
   sounds objective and is not. Confidence on a citation is a researcher's
   grading, so this is the AI acting on its own self-report. It also needs the
   most explaining for the least gain.

## What each choice changes

- **Option 1** changes nothing about permissions. AWF-WO-012 builds the proposal
  record, the review surface, and the batch-confirm path. No consent copy moves,
  no new scope.
- **Option 2** requires a new scope or a widened `research:write`, a rewritten
  consent screen, a revised `NEVER_PERMITTED` list, an undo path, and a
  re-verification of `check:agent-quality-gates`. It is a release with a
  user-visible permissions change, not a feature.
- **Option 3** requires everything Option 2 requires, plus a threshold policy
  Scott has to be willing to defend to a user whose great-grandmother's birth
  year was changed by a machine.

## Safe default

Option 1. It is already what the code and the consent screen do today, so
choosing it costs nothing and unblocks AWF-WO-012 immediately.

## Consequence of waiting

Small and bounded, which is the point of having built the person half first. A
person can already resolve conflicts today, and an AI can already see them and
write a proposal as a research finding. Waiting only delays the *structured*
proposal surface. Nothing is broken while this sits, and no work already done
needs to be undone under any of the three options.
