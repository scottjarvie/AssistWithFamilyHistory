## Why this exists

The Personal AI scenario this product is measured against is: build a
source-grounded deceased ancestor branch, **resolve a conflict**, and create a
family story packet. Two of those three are real today. The middle one is not.

Conflict is the ordinary condition of genealogy, not an edge case. A census
says 1847, a headstone says 1849, a parish register says 1848. The work *is*
deciding which record to believe and writing down why. A research tool that can
only say "these disagree" has handed the hardest part back to the person with no
place to record the answer.

## What is genuinely strong here

This is not a missing-foundation problem. The evidence model is the best-built
part of the vault and it is unusually rigorous for a product this young:

- `sources` (`convex/schema.ts:392`) carries `repository`, `url`, `author`,
  `publicationDate`, and a 14-value `type`.
- `citations` (`:455`) separates **evidence from conclusion** with `isEvidence`
  (`:460`), grades belief on a five-level `confidence` scale (`:465`), and keeps
  `extractedText` alongside `editedText` (`:474`) plus `accessDate` (`:479`).
- `citationLinks` (`:499`) is the fact→source pointer: a citation attaches to a
  person, relationship, event, or place, down to the specific `field` it
  supports (`:509`).
- `sourceFacts` (`:526`) are candidate facts born already attached to their
  citation, and they deliberately do not overwrite canonical person fields.

That is a real Source/Citation separation, which most hobby tools never build.
The gap below is a missing *move*, not a missing *model*.

## The gap, precisely

**Detection exists, but only on one path and only for three fact types.**
`findSourceFactConflicts()` (`lib/familysearch/sourceFacts.ts:80-124`) compares
an imported indexed fact against the capture header for name, birth, and death
only. It runs solely on the FamilySearch capture-import path
(`lib/familysearch/importer.ts:84-88`). Nothing anywhere compares two citations
already sitting in the vault against each other — which is the case a
researcher hits constantly.

**Flagging works.** The importer downgrades the fact to `confidence: "low"`,
sets `status: "conflict"` and a `conflictReason`
(`lib/familysearch/importer.ts:142-144`), and opens a `researchTasks` row of
type `conflict_resolution` (`:275-284`). The person workspace renders the badge
and the reason (`app/app/people/[personId]/page.tsx:508-527`).

**Resolution does not exist.** `upsertSourceFact`
(`convex/vaultMutations.ts:1091`) is the only writer of `sourceFacts.status`,
and its only non-MCP caller is the importer itself
(`lib/familysearch/importer.ts:131`). There is no `acceptSourceFact`,
`rejectSourceFact`, or `resolveConflict` mutation, and no click-path in the
person workspace that moves a fact from `conflict` to `accepted` or `rejected`.
The `conflict_resolution` task can be closed, but closing it changes nothing
about the fact that caused it. So the badge is permanent: the vault can enter
the conflict state and cannot leave it.

**`citations.conflictsWith` is write-only in practice.** It is declared
(`convex/schema.ts:483`) and validated on write (`convex/citations.ts:81-89`,
`:441`), and then never read by any surface. The one field designed to say
"these two records disagree" is currently a value the product stores and never
shows anyone.

**The AI is told the wrong thing about conflicts.**
`convex/contextPackBuilder.ts:173` reads:

```ts
unresolvedConflicts: storyClaimReadiness.unresolvedImportWarnings,
```

The field a connected AI would reasonably read as "the unresolved conflicts" is
populated with import warnings, not with the `sourceFacts` rows whose status is
actually `conflict`. An AI asked to help resolve a conflict is handed a list
that does not contain the conflicts. This one is a naming-and-wiring defect on
the flagship path, and it is small.

## Current / Partial / Later

- **Current:** the Source/Citation/citationLink/sourceFact model, conflict
  *detection* on the FamilySearch import path for name/birth/death, the conflict
  badge and reason in the person workspace, and the auto-opened
  `conflict_resolution` research task.
- **Partial:** the conflict lifecycle. A conflict can be created and displayed
  but never settled, and the AI-facing `unresolvedConflicts` field does not
  carry conflicts.
- **Later:** general in-vault conflict detection between any two citations,
  conflict types beyond name/birth/death, and any FamilySearch-side conflict
  resolution (`familySearchSync.syncStatus: "conflict"`, `convex/schema.ts:787`,
  is declared with no detector behind it).

## Smallest useful next step

Two bounded pieces, in this order, and the first is worth doing on its own:

1. **Correct what the AI is told.** Either populate `unresolvedConflicts` from
   the `sourceFacts` rows with `status: "conflict"` (carrying `factType`,
   `value`, `conflictReason`, and the citation behind each side), or rename the
   field to `unresolvedImportWarnings` so it stops claiming to be something it
   is not. Populating it is the better answer, because the AI needs that list to
   do the scenario at all. This touches `convex/contextPackBuilder.ts` and
   `scripts/check-context-pack-contract.ts`.

2. **Give the conflict a closing move.** An owner-scoped mutation that sets a
   conflicting `sourceFact` to `accepted` or `rejected`, records the reasoning
   as durable research text, and leaves the losing reading visible rather than
   deleted — a resolved conflict is evidence about the researcher's judgment and
   should not vanish. Genealogical practice is that you never discard the record
   you decided against.

   Note the authority question before building: `family_history_save_records`
   and `save_source_evidence` already let a chosen AI write `status: "conflict"`
   (`convex/httpRoutes/mcp.ts:292`, `:405`). Whether an AI may also *resolve* a
   conflict, or only propose a resolution for the person to confirm, is a
   product decision for Scott, not an implementation detail. Resolving a
   conflict is the researcher's judgment call, so the safe default is that the
   AI proposes and the person confirms.

## Definition of done

- A conflicting `sourceFact` can be moved to `accepted` or `rejected` by its
  owner through an owner-scoped mutation, and the losing reading remains visible
  in the vault rather than being deleted.
- The reasoning for the decision is recorded as durable research text, so a
  reader a year later can see why one record was believed over another.
- The auto-opened `conflict_resolution` research task closes as a consequence of
  the resolution rather than independently of it.
- `unresolvedConflicts` in the AI context pack either carries the actual
  `sourceFacts` conflict rows or no longer uses that name.
- Whether a chosen AI may resolve, or only propose, is decided by Scott and
  written into `lib/mcp/catalog.ts` guidance and this Card before build.
- `pnpm verify` passes, and `scripts/check-context-pack-contract.ts` covers the
  corrected field.
