## Why this exists

A private family-history Queue must never rely on a deployment mode to decide
whether an anonymous or cross-owner caller may continue. An expired AI handoff
must also stop authority predictably and remain recoverable without leaving a
stale deadline that immediately blocks the newly chosen AI.

## Current truth

The 2026-08-12 independent audit reproduced that public Queue functions used
the shared tenant guard whose legacy `shadow` mode logs a denial but returns a
decision. It also found that the expiry mutation had no scheduler/read wiring,
AI continuation checked only lease expiry after claim, and assign/resume/reopen
could retain an expired handoff deadline. The focused repair is implemented on
`codex/family-history-queue-authority-repair` and passes local adversarial
Convex, pure contract, and TypeScript checks. It is not merged, deployed,
provider-proven, or independently audited yet.

## Scope

- Add a Queue-local fail-closed guard for every public Queue read and mutation
  without changing the broader trust-boundary provider policy.
- Schedule idempotent lifecycle expiry for new handoff/lease deadlines and
  reconcile missed or pre-existing deadlines during bounded reads.
- Prevent chosen-AI leases and continuation commands from outliving handoff
  authority.
- Make assign, resume, and reopen clear or explicitly renew stale expiry while
  preserving honest disconnected behavior and exactly four product states.
- Add adversarial Convex fixtures for anonymous/cross-owner shadow-mode calls,
  scheduler/read reconciliation, bounded authority, and recovery.

## Next safe action

Complete full local verification, publish the focused protected pull request,
