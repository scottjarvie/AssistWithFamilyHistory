## Why this exists

Signing in proves which private vault belongs to the person. It does not answer
which Family History records and operations that person allowed this AI client
to use. The product needs one durable, owner-visible grant between OAuth
identity and every tool call.

## Current truth

The MCP edge verifies issuer, signature, OAuth access-token type, expiry,
subject, and client identifier, then derives the vault owner server-side. It
parses token scopes, but domain tools do not check them. Queue tools receive a
hard-coded full Queue scope set after login. The live Clerk provider advertises
identity scopes, not Family History record/operation scopes.

`agentActivity` records successful MCP writes, but there is no connection grant,
read/denial activity, owner-facing connection inventory, selected record
boundary, expiry, or active-grant check. Client deletion also does not
immediately invalidate an already issued JWT.

## Requirements

- Store an owner/client grant with selected record boundary, operation scopes,
  consent, issued/last-used/expires/revoked times, status, and safe provenance.
- Resolve the grant from verified OAuth subject and client identity; accept no
  owner, workspace, vault, or grant selector from the client.
- Enforce explicit `context:read`, `evidence:read`, `research:write`,
  `story:draft`, `queue:read`, and `queue:work` families at every tool.
- Keep archive/restore separate and keep publish, delete, merge, sharing,
  export, and provider action outside the initial server ceiling.
- Fail discovery and calls promptly after expiry or revocation, without leaking
  whether an out-of-bound record exists.
- Record safe read, write, denial, and revoke activity without copying private
  evidence or result content into telemetry.

## Next safe action

After Scott approves AWF-WO-011 and the AWF-0034/AWF-0038 architecture choice,
write the grant/scope threat model and scope-to-tool matrix before changing
schema, provider, or token handling.

## Definition of done

- [ ] Every MCP tool names and enforces its minimum product scope.
- [ ] OAuth identity without an active matching grant cannot list or call tools.
- [ ] Record boundaries and Queue assignment are independently enforced.
- [ ] Expired and revoked grants fail within the documented short bound.
- [ ] The owner can inspect scopes, boundary, activity, last use, and status.
- [ ] Two-owner and out-of-grant probes fail without record-existence leakage.
- [ ] Source/local, PR/CI, deployment, live, client, cleanup, and audit proof are
      recorded separately.

## Boundaries

- No provider setting, production token format, client registration, secret,
  account, real-family record, billing, DNS, or deployment change without the
  Work Order's explicit later gate.
- Do not reuse API-key presets as OAuth grants without proving their semantics,
  lifecycle, and server enforcement are the same.

## Evidence

- `convex/httpRoutes/mcp.ts` parses `auth.scopes` but does not authorize domain
  tools from them; it constructs the Queue principal with four scopes.
- `convex/schema.ts` has API keys, activity, MCP receipts, and record keys, but
  no OAuth connection/grant table.
- `docs/planning/family-history-bring-your-ai-alignment.md` records the complete
  audit and target scope vocabulary.
